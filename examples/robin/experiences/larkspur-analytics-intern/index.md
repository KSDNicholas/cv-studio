# Data Analyst Intern, Larkspur Freight

## Context

- Larkspur Freight is a regional trucking and warehousing company with about 400 staff. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- I sat in the operations analytics team of 3 analysts and reported to the head of operations analytics. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- 12-week summer internship, full-time. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- The operations managers were tracking late deliveries in a shared Excel file that someone updated by hand every Monday. (Robin, 2026-09-20)

## What I did

- I wrote the SQL that pulls delivery records from the company's PostgreSQL warehouse into one clean table, one row per delivery, about 1.2 million rows covering 2024 and 2025. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- I built a Power BI dashboard on top of that table showing on-time delivery rate by depot, route and week. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- The dashboard refreshes every morning on a schedule, so the Monday Excel update stopped. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- I ran an analysis of why deliveries were late. I joined delivery records to driver shift data and found that 38% of late deliveries came from routes with a loading time over 45 minutes at the depot. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- I presented the loading time finding to the head of operations and 4 depot managers in week 9. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- Two depots changed their loading schedule after the presentation. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- I wrote documentation for the delivery table so the analysts could maintain the SQL after I left. (Robin, 2026-09-20)

## Numbers

- On-time delivery at the two depots that changed their loading schedule went from 87% to 92% over the following 6 weeks. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- The dashboard had about 25 weekly users by the end of the internship. (Robin, 2026-09-20)
- The Monday Excel update had taken one analyst about 3 hours a week. (Robin, 2026-09-20)

## Tools

- SQL (PostgreSQL), Power BI, Excel, Python (pandas) for the late delivery analysis. (Robin, 2026-09-20)

## What went wrong and what I changed

- My first version of the dashboard counted a delivery as late when it was split across two trucks. A depot manager spotted the numbers looked too high. I fixed the SQL to count by order and added a check that compares my totals with the finance team's monthly delivery count. (Robin, 2026-09-20)

## Interview story

- The loading time finding. Everyone assumed traffic caused late deliveries, but the data showed the delay started at the depot. (Robin, 2026-09-20)

## Target role angle

- This is the closest thing I have to a real data analyst job: SQL, a dashboard people used, and an analysis that changed a decision. (Robin, 2026-09-20)

## Shareability
